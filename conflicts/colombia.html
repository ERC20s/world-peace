Overview

Colombia's internal armed conflict, involving the state, left-wing guerrilla groups including the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (FARC-EP) and the National Liberation Army (ELN), right-wing paramilitary groups, and drug-trafficking organisations, lasted more than five decades and is estimated to have killed over 220,000 people and displaced millions. After four years of formal talks in Havana, the Colombian government and the FARC-EP signed the Final Agreement for Ending the Conflict and Building a Stable and Lasting Peace in November 2016, following a renegotiation after the original text was narrowly rejected in an October 2016 plebiscite. Implementation continues and is monitored by a UN mission; violence from FARC dissident factions, the ELN, and other armed groups persists in parts of the country. This page lists neutral, verifiable initiatives and organisations and sources for further reading.

Initiatives and organisations

  • Jurisdicción Especial para la Paz (Special Jurisdiction for Peace, JEP) — a transitional-justice tribunal created by the 2016 Final Agreement to investigate and judge serious crimes committed during the conflict, offering reduced sanctions in exchange for truth and reparation. Source.
  • Comisión para el Esclarecimiento de la Verdad (Truth Commission) — an independent body mandated by the Final Agreement to clarify patterns and causes of the conflict; it operated from 2018 and published its final report in 2022. Source.
  • CINEP/Programa por la Paz — an independent Colombian research centre that documents human rights violations and conflict dynamics and publishes the long-running Noche y Niebla database. Source.
  • United Nations Verification Mission in Colombia — a UN Security Council-mandated mission that verifies implementation of the 2016 Final Agreement, including reincorporation of former combatants and security guarantees. Source.

What a reader can do today

  • Read primary documents such as the 2016 Final Agreement and the Truth Commission's final report to understand the process directly.
  • Follow verified reporting from the UN Verification Mission and established research organisations rather than unverified social-media claims.
